Jiayi Li

Pronounced roughly “jyah-yee lee”

I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Section of Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence at MPI-CBG in Dresden. I received my PhD in Statistics from UCLA, where I was a member of the Mathematical Machine Learning Group.

I study the geometry and statistics of machine learning: what models can represent, how they learn and generalize, and what can be inferred from data. Using algebraic geometry and singular learning theory, I connect model structure with statistical complexity and identifiability. These questions also guide my current work on interpretability and AI alignment.

Research

Geometry & statistics

What does a model’s structure make possible?

I study how architecture shapes the geometry of functions represented by polynomial neural networks, and which changes in the data topological representations detect.

Learning

How does this structure influence what is learned?

Solutions that fit the same data can have different local complexity. I use singular learning theory to connect this geometry to generalization, including grokking in shallow quadratic networks.

Guarantees

What can the data identify?

A good fit alone does not ensure that model parameters can be recovered. My work establishes identifiability conditions for graphical dynamical models using information beyond covariance.

Teaching & mentoring

At UCLA, I taught statistics, data science, and engineering, and led the preparatory course for incoming master’s students. I mentor students from undergraduate to PhD level on theoretical and empirical questions in machine learning, helping them formulate problems, assess evidence, and think independently.

Academic service

I co-organize meetings on singular learning theory and algebraic methods in machine learning, including JMM special sessions and a SIAM MDS minisymposium. I served as Editor in Chief of ACM XRDS and helped establish UCLA’s Distinguished Women in Statistics and Data Science workshops.
