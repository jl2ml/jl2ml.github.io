Research

The geometry behind how learning systems behave.

Modern neural networks are singular statistical models: symmetries, degeneracies, and non-identifiability are not edge cases but central features. I develop geometric and statistical tools that turn this structure into explanations, predictions, and guarantees.

Structure

What functions can a network represent, which parameters are recoverable, and what geometric invariants survive reparameterization?

Neuroalgebraic geometry

Geometry of polynomial neural networks

The weights of a polynomial network parametrize a semialgebraic function space. Its dimension measures expressivity, while the algebraic degree of its closure captures aspects of learning complexity. This gives a coordinate-independent vocabulary for comparing architectures.

Algebraic statistics

Identifiability in graphical discrete Lyapunov models

For directed systems at steady state, higher-order cumulants can recover information that covariance alone cannot. We establish generic and local identifiability results and connect them to the graph encoded by the system's parameter matrix.

Geometric data analysis

Pull-back geometry of persistent-homology encodings

The Jacobian of an encoding induces a geometry on the data manifold. Measuring perturbations in this geometry shows which features a persistent-homology representation recognizes or ignores—without first training a downstream model.

Dynamics

How does training move among qualitatively different solutions, and which local quantities reveal a change of learning regime?

Singular learning theory

A basin-selection perspective on grokking

We view grokking as a transition between competing near-zero-loss basins. The local learning coefficient ranks the basins by statistical preference, while optimization dynamics determine when training moves between them. In quadratic networks, this perspective yields closed-form results and an empirical probe of the path to generalization.

Current direction

Local complexity along the optimization path

I am interested in using singular invariants—not only endpoint loss—to describe how representations and effective model complexity evolve during training. The goal is to connect rigorous asymptotics with quantities that can be estimated in contemporary learning systems.

03

Guarantees

Which conclusions about interpretability, generalization, and robustness are invariant—and therefore candidates for proof?

Reliable AI

Identifiability before interpretation

An explanation tied to a non-identifiable coordinate system can change while the learned function remains the same. My agenda treats symmetry, equivalence, and identifiability as prerequisites for claims about what a model has learned.

Resilient systems

Predicting vulnerability and recovering from damage

Differential geometry can locate vulnerable directions in a trained network and construct recovery paths after parameters are damaged. This early work motivates a broader question: how can geometric structure support guarantees for systems deployed under perturbation?

The through line

Structure → dynamics → guarantees

These themes are not separate projects. The structure of a model shapes its loss landscape; that landscape shapes learning dynamics; and invariant structure determines which explanations and guarantees can be trusted.
