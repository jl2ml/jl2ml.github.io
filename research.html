Research agenda

How structure becomes behavior in learning systems.

Modern neural networks are singular statistical models. Symmetry and failures of identifiability shape both what they can represent and how they learn. I use algebraic geometry, singular learning theory, and statistics to study the chain from model structure, through training dynamics, to the invariant claims needed for interpretability, reliability, and AI alignment.

Structure

What functions can a network represent, and which geometric quantities describe that function space independently of coordinates?

Neuroalgebraic geometry · 2024

Geometry of polynomial neural networks

How does architecture shape the geometry of representable functions?

The weights of a polynomial network parametrize a semialgebraic function space. Its dimension measures expressivity, while its learning degree captures an algebraic aspect of training complexity. Together, these invariants provide a vocabulary for comparing architectures that is independent of coordinates.

With Kaie Kubjas and Maximilian Wiesmann

Geometric data analysis · 2024

Pull-back geometry of persistent homology encodings

What does a topological representation actually see?

The Jacobian of an encoding induces a geometry on the data manifold. Measuring perturbations in this geometry reveals which features a persistent homology representation recognizes or ignores. This analysis does not require training a downstream model first.

Dynamics

How does training move among qualitatively different solutions, and which local quantities reveal a change of learning regime?

Singular learning theory · 2026

A basin-selection perspective on grokking

Why can generalization arrive long after a network has fit its training data?

We view grokking as a transition between competing basins whose losses are nearly zero. The local learning coefficient ranks the basins by statistical preference, while optimization dynamics determine when training moves between them. In quadratic networks, this perspective yields explicit results and an empirical probe of the path to generalization.

With Ben Cullen, Sergio Estan-Ruiz, and Riya Danait

Current direction · Singular learning theory

Tracking phase transitions during training

Can local singular complexity reveal a change of learning regime before aggregate loss does?

I am developing ways to use singular invariants alongside endpoint loss to describe how representations and effective model complexity evolve during training. The goal is to connect rigorous asymptotics with quantities that can be estimated in contemporary learning systems and related to changes in behavior that matter for alignment.

03

Guarantees

Which properties of a learned system are invariant under reparameterization, and when can we prove that they are recoverable, robust, or meaningful?

AI alignment · Current direction

Identifiability before interpretation

When is an explanation a property of the learned function rather than an artifact of its coordinates?

An explanation tied to a parameterization that is not identifiable can change while the learned function remains the same. My agenda treats symmetry, equivalence, and identifiability as prerequisites for rigorous claims about what a model has learned. These conditions also help determine which internal features can support reliable oversight.

Algebraic statistics · 2026

Identifiability in graphical discrete Lyapunov models

When can a directed mechanism be recovered from observations at steady state?

Third and fourth cumulants reveal parameters that covariance alone cannot. We establish generic and local identifiability results and connect them to the directed graph encoded by the system's parameter matrix.

With Cecilie Olesen Recke, Sarah Lumpp, Nataliia Kushnerchuk, Janike Oldekop, Jane Ivy Coons, and Elina Robeva

Resilient systems · 2020

Predicting vulnerability and recovering from damage

Can geometry predict where a trained network will fail under damage?

Differential geometry can locate vulnerable directions in a trained network and construct recovery paths after parameters are damaged. This early work motivates a broader question: how can geometric structure support guarantees for systems deployed under perturbation?

With Guruprasad Raghavan and Matt Thomson

The through line

Structure → dynamics → guarantees

These themes are not separate projects. A model's structure determines the solutions available to training; learning dynamics select among them; and invariant structure determines which explanations, interventions, and guarantees can be trusted.
